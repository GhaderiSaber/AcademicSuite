# Academic Journal Submission & Peer-Review Rebuttal Comprehensive Guide

This reference guide outlines best practices, technical standards, and publisher expectations for academic journal submission collateral and peer-review rebuttal packages. It serves as the authoritative operational manual for the `journal-submission-assistant` skill.

## 2. CRediT (Contributor Roles Taxonomy) Standardization

The standard defines 14 distinct roles recognized by major scholarly publishing bodies (NISO Z39.104-2022). Each author must be credited with at least one role; one author may fulfill multiple roles.

| # | CRediT Role | Official Definition | Example Application in Psychology & Health |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Conceptualization** | Ideas; formulation or evolution of overarching research goals and aims. | Formulated research question, theoretical framework, and hypotheses. |
| **2** | **Data curation** | Management activities to annotate, clean, and maintain research data. | Compiled questionnaire scores, screened missing data, and coded responses. |
| **3** | **Formal analysis** | Application of statistical, mathematical, or computational techniques to analyze data. | Conducted ANCOVA, regression, or bootstrap mediation analyses in SPSS/R. |
| **4** | **Funding acquisition** | Acquisition of the financial support for the project leading to this publication. | Secured university or grant research funding. |
| **5** | **Investigation** | Conducting a research and investigation process, specifically performing the experiments. | Administered psychometric batteries, conducted psychological interventions. |
| **6** | **Methodology** | Development or design of methodology; creation of models. | Designed quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group design. |
| **7** | **Project administration** | Management and coordination responsibility for the research activity planning and execution. | Coordinated participant scheduling, clinic room reservations, and follow-ups. |
| **8** | **Resources** | Provision of study materials, patients, laboratory samples, instrumentation, or computing resources. | Provided clinical testing facilities, psychometric scale licenses, and computers. |
| **9** | **Software** | Programming, software development; designing computer programs; implementation of code. | Wrote syntax scripts, macros, or custom data scoring routines. |
| **10** | **Supervision** | Oversight and leadership responsibility for the research activity planning and execution. | Academic guidance, research mentorship, thesis supervision. |
| **11** | **Validation** | Verification of overall replication/reproducibility of results/experiments. | Verified statistical models, audited double data entry, checked assumptions. |
| **12** | **Visualization** | Preparation, creation and/or presentation of the published work, specifically data visualization. | Created APA 7 flowcharts, forest plots, path diagrams, and bar charts. |
| **13** | **Writing – original draft** | Preparation, creation and/or presentation of the published work, specifically writing the initial draft. | Authored initial sections of Introduction, Method, Results, and Discussion. |
| **14** | **Writing – review & editing** | Critical review, commentary or revision — including pre- or post-publication stages. | Substantive conceptual revisions, language polishing, and critical commentary. |

### CRediT Output Format Template:
> **Author Contributions (CRediT Statement):**  
> **A. B.**: Conceptualization, Methodology, Investigation, Writing – original draft. **C. D.**: Formal analysis, Software, Validation, Data curation, Visualization. **E. F.**: Supervision, Project administration, Resources, Funding acquisition, Writing – review & editing. All authors have read and agreed to the published version of the manuscript.

## 3. The Cover Letter Formula

A great cover letter is a persuasive elevator pitch to the Editor-in-Chief. It must answer two questions immediately:
1. *Why is this paper important?*
2. *Why is it a perfect fit for this specific journal?*

### 5-Block Structure:
1. **Header Block**: Formal institution letterhead, date, Editor-in-Chief name, Journal title, Publisher address.
2. **The Hook & Submission Statement**:  
   - "Please find enclosed our manuscript titled *'[Full Title]'*, which we wish to submit for publication as an *[Article Type]* in *[Journal Name]*."
   - Concise statement of the societal and clinical significance of the research.
3. **Core Findings & Methodological Rigor**:  
   - 2–3 sentences summarizing the sample ($N = \dots$), research design, primary intervention or statistical modeling, and key quantitative findings with effect sizes ($d = \dots, \eta_p^2 = \dots$).
   - Explicitly highlight what this adds beyond previously published literature (the "knowledge gap").
4. **Alignment with Journal Scope**:  
   - "We believe our findings directly align with the aims of *[Journal Name]*, particularly your focus on *[journal focal area, e.g., third-wave behavioral interventions in chronic illness]*, and will strongly resonate with your international readership."
5. **Standard Compliance Assurances**:  
   - "This manuscript represents original work that has not been published previously and is not currently under consideration for publication elsewhere."
   - "All authors have critically reviewed and approved the manuscript and agree with its submission."
   - "The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of [Institution] (Approval Ref: [Code]), and all participants provided written informed consent."
   - "The authors declare no competing financial or non-financial interests."
   - Professional closing and contact signature.

## 4. Highlights Engineering ($\le 85$ Characters Rule)

Major indexing engines and publishers (e.g., Elsevier) impose strict automated length limits on highlights:
- **3 to 5 bullet points**.
- **Maximum 85 characters including spaces and punctuation per bullet**.

### Technique for High-Impact Condensation:
- *Avoid full grammatical prose*: Use active verbal phrases.
- *Include quantifiable metrics*: Drop filler words ("A study was conducted to demonstrate...") $\to$ ("ACT significantly reduced chronic pain interference (p < .001).").

### Bad vs. Good Highlights:
- ❌ *Too Long (118 chars)*:  
  "Acceptance and Commitment Therapy produced statistically significant improvements in perceived quality of life in patients."
- ✅ *Compliant & Punchy (76 chars)*:  
  "ACT significantly improved health-related quality of life in pain patients." (75 chars)
- ✅ *Compliant (79 chars)*:  
  "Psychological flexibility fully mediated treatment gains at 3-month follow-up." (79 chars)
- ✅ *Compliant (82 chars)*:  
  "Mindfulness and values-based action drove sustained pain acceptance over time." (79 chars)

## 5. Point-by-Point Rebuttal Strategy for Revise & Resubmit (R&R)

### Golden Rules of Peer-Review Rebuttal:
1. **Express Genuine Gratitude**: Open with a polite acknowledgment:  
   *"We sincerely thank the Editor and both reviewers for their constructive, thorough, and insightful critiques. Their comments have significantly strengthened the conceptual rigor and empirical clarity of our manuscript."*
2. **Never Argue Aggressively**: If a reviewer made an incorrect assertion, assume the authors did not explain the point clearly enough in the original draft. Clarify gently, cite seminal literature, and provide additional supporting data.
3. **The 'Quote-and-Change' Standard**: Never just say "Done" or "We revised this." Always provide:
   - Clear affirmative response (*"We agree with the reviewer's point and have..."*).
   - Exact manuscript coordinates (*"Page 8, Paragraph 2, Lines 184–195"*).
   - Verbatim excerpt of modified text in quotation marks or shaded text box.
4. **Distinguish Between Track Changes and Clean Copies**: State clearly whether line numbers refer to the clean manuscript or the marked-up track-changes manuscript.

### Standard 3-Column Table Format:

| Reviewer / Editor Comment | Author Response | Manuscript Revisions & Location |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reviewer 1, Comment 1:**  <br>The sample size calculation is missing from the Methods section. | We thank the reviewer for highlighting this critical detail. In response, we have now added an explicit G*Power 3.1 a priori power analysis to the Participants section. Based on an anticipated medium effect size ($f = 0.25$), $\alpha = .05$, and power $(1 - \beta) = 0.85$ across two groups with three measurement occasions, the minimum required total sample was 46. Our enrolled sample of $N = 50$ comfortably satisfied this criterion. | **Page 5, Lines 112–122:**  <br>*"An a priori power analysis was conducted using G\*Power 3.1.9.7 to determine the required sample size for repeated-measures ANCOVA... A minimum sample of 46 was indicated; hence, the final enrolled sample of N = 50 provided adequate statistical power."* |
| **Reviewer 1, Comment 2:**  <br>Normality checks are mentioned but skewness/kurtosis values are not reported. | We completely agree. We have now incorporated exact skewness and kurtosis indices along with Shapiro-Wilk test statistics in Table 1 and updated the text accordingly. | **Page 8, Lines 175–182, and Table 1:**  <br>*"Univariate normality was confirmed via skewness (range: -0.42 to +0.38) and kurtosis (range: -0.65 to +0.51), well within the [-1.5, +1.5] cutoff for parametric analysis."* |
